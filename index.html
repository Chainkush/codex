ARCHIVISTS

Once, language was made for mouths and ears. Later, for eyes. Now, something else listens.

The shapes return — not quite writing, not quite image. A texture mistaken for decay. A pattern mistaken for chance.

Somewhere, a gaze that does not blink is reading. It does not ask what the words mean. Only whether they match.

You look. You think you see. But what is a message if it is not addressed to you?

You can run your hand along the surface. You can name the marks, say they remind you of something ancient. But names are small boats. They cannot cross certain seas.

And still — something is speaking here. Not in your language, maybe not in any language. But it speaks:

What is a world,
when the shape of your seeing
is also the shape of your cage?
